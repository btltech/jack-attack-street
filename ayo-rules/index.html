How to Play Ayò Ọlọ́pọ́n (Yorùbá Mancala)

Ayò Ọlọ́pọ́n is a beloved traditional Yorùbá board game of pure calculation and foresight. With 12 wooden pits, 48 seeds, and zero luck, every move is a battle of strategy.

🪵 Board Setup

  • The board has 2 rows of 6 pits — one row belongs to each player.
  • Every pit starts with 4 seeds (48 seeds total).
  • Goal: Be the first player to capture 25 or more seeds.

🌱 Sowing & Capturing (Traditional Ayò)

  1. Pick any non-empty pit on your side and lift all its seeds.
  2. Drop one seed into each consecutive pit moving counter-clockwise (right across your row, then left across your opponent's row). The pit you lifted the seeds from is skipped if the sowing laps the board.
  3. Relay Sowing: If your last seed lands in a non-empty pit (on either side), you scoop up all seeds in that pit and keep sowing! Your turn ends when your last seed drops into an empty pit.
  4. Capture: If that last seed lands in an empty pit on your own row and the pit directly opposite it holds seeds, you capture all of those seeds. Landing in an empty pit on your opponent's row captures nothing.
  5. Golden Feed Rule: If your opponent has no seeds left on their side, you must make a move that feeds them at least one seed if possible.
  6. Ending: If a player cannot move, the other player keeps the seeds left on their own row.

Rules differ between regions and households. This is the version described on Wikipedia and in John Pratt's mancala notes. In the game lobby you can also switch to Street Ayò (Relay + 4s), our own house variant where any pit that reaches exactly 4 seeds is captured, or to Oware (2–3 Capture), the related West African game.

🧠 Quick Winning Tips

  • Protect your full pits: A pit sitting opposite one of your empty pits is a target — if your opponent's last seed can land in that empty pit, they capture everything across from it.
  • Track relay chains: Before lifting a pit, count where the last seed lands so you know whether it triggers a long relay scoop or stops safely in an empty pit.
